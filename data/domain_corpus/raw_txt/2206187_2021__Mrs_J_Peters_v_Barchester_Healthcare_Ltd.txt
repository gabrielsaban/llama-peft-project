JUDGMENT
1. The claimant’s complaint of indirect sex discrimination is dismissed upon
her withdrawal of it.
2. The claimant’s complaints of direct discrimination because of belief at
allegations (paragraphs) 25, 26, 28, 29, 31, 33 and 34 of her particulars of
claim are dismissed upon her withdrawal of them.
3. The claimant’s complaint of direct discrimination because of belief and
harassment related to belief at allegation 45 of her particulars of claim are
struck out as having no reasonable prospects of success.
4. The claimant’s claim of unfair dismissal is struck out (in part) as having no
reasonable prospect of success save insofar as she maintains that the
decision to dismiss was unreasonable on the following grounds:
 that as a senior employee and member of the senior
management team, she was not covered by the respondent’s
policy or ought reasonably not to have been,
 she worked in a position where contact with care home
residents was never required (she says that she was not a risk
to residents)
 she worked remotely from care homes

Case No: 2206187/2021 & 2201889/2022
 any meeting involving a care home which she might have had
to undertake could have been conducted remotely
 there had been no evaluation of the effect of non-frontline care
workers not being vaccinated
 procedural unfairness in the process of investigation,
dismissal and her appeal, including in respect of her dismissal
having been announced before the determination of her
appeal
 her dismissal being tainted by any acts found to have been
direct discrimination because of belief or harassment related
to belief
For the avoidance of doubt the claim of unfair dismissal is struck out (in part) so as
to preclude any argument by the claimant that the respondent’s vaccine policy
involved any interference with her human rights in terms of security of the person
or her pleaded protected belief.
